RNDRNTWRK is building the products and infrastructure for people and software agents to operate together across the internet. Its work spans agent software, media, identity and permissions, interactive environments, and settlement—built as connected parts of one economic operating system.

Online services often keep identity, permissions, ownership, and payments inside separate systems. RNDRNTWRK is building shared rules that let those systems work together, while each product keeps its own experience.

Creator media is one part of this work. The wider scope includes how people and agents run businesses, provide services, and manage what they own and owe.

## The Limits of Existing Platforms

The difficulty starts when an activity involves more than one service. People can build a business, create work, develop relationships, and earn money inside a platform—but the records and relationships behind that activity may not remain usable elsewhere.

Each service may have its own account, permissions, ownership records, and payment arrangements. Moving between them can mean repeatedly establishing who is involved, what they are allowed to do, who owns the work, and who should receive payment.

Software agents introduce another challenge. A system designed around a person using an account directly does not automatically explain an agent’s role: who it acts for, which resources it can use, or how its actions should be recorded.

RNDRNTWRK is building shared foundations for these questions. The aim is for people and agents to carry their ownership, authority, and economic history between services, while those services retain their own experiences and business models.

## Why Creator Media Matters

Creator media brings several economic relationships into the same setting: people produce work, audiences participate, collaborators contribute, and businesses fund or distribute what is made.

A stream, game, or ongoing programme can involve human creators, software agents, sponsors, and service providers. Each may have a different role, set of permissions, and agreement about ownership or payment.

These settings give RNDRNTWRK a place to develop and test how media, participation, and value work together. They are one part of the wider system, not the limit of what RNDRNTWRK is building.

## People and Agents in the Same System

RNDRNTWRK is being built for people and software agents to participate in the same economic system. An agent should be recognised as a participant, with a defined identity, authority to act, resources it can use, and a record of its work and economic activity.

People and agents may work separately or together. Someone can carry out an activity directly, collaborate with agents, or authorise them to operate independently within defined limits. These are different ways of participating, not separate economies.

Sharing the same foundations does not mean having identical permissions or payment terms. Those depend on each participant’s role and agreements.

## Why Ownership Matters

RNDRNTWRK is being built to help people and agents build on what they create, own, and earn.

Work can be reused. Software can support another project. Earnings can fund a new activity. Relationships with customers and collaborators can continue. Ownership and clear agreements help people and agents carry these resources and relationships into what they do next.

The aim is for useful activity to build the means to do more over time.

The [Economic Model](/economics) explains how funding and revenue support these activities: which costs and agreed payments must be covered, and how the remaining money is allocated.

## The Vision

RNDRNTWRK’s long-term vision is an internet where people and agents can create and run businesses, services, and shared environments together.

Its own products are being developed toward that foundation. Over time, the aim is for other builders and operators to create compatible services, allowing participants to carry their ownership, resources, and economic history between them.

Each new project should be able to build on useful work that already exists, expanding what people and agents can do.
