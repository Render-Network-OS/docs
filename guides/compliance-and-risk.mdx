## Before Participating

Start with the terms of the product or programme you intend to use. They explain the activity offered, the parties involved, any access conditions, and how questions or problems can be raised.

Read those terms alongside the activity’s costs, permissions, funding arrangements, and treatment of unfinished work or payments. Keep a copy of the terms relevant to your participation.

For a legal, financial, or tax decision that depends on your circumstances, take the relevant product and programme information to a qualified adviser.

## Access Requirements

All you need to get started is an internet-enabled device.

A particular service or programme may set additional conditions for participation. Check its published age requirements, supported locations, account rules, and any required verification before using that feature.

Signing in with a wallet establishes an account interaction; it does not explain every condition that may apply to an activity. Use the access instructions for the selected service, and ask through its support path if a requirement is unclear.

People and agents can participate under different roles. An experience’s rules define what each may do and whose authority an agent uses.

## Identity Checks and Personal Information

If a service requests identity information, review its verification instructions and privacy information before submitting it. These should explain:

- Which organisation is requesting the information and how to reach it.
- Why the information is needed and what must be supplied.
- How the information is handled, shared, and retained.
- How to ask a question or raise a problem with the verification process.

Use the verification path identified by that service. A request from a third party needs to be considered under that party’s own terms and data-handling information.

## Risk Categories

### Operations and settlement

Software errors, service interruptions, and integration failures can affect session results, access, or settlement. Before an economic action, review the recipient, asset, amount, network, costs, and the status information the service provides.

Check how an interrupted operation is followed up and whether an action can be cancelled or corrected. Retain the operation reference and result so that a pending request can be distinguished from a completed payment.

### Funds and market conditions

A token balance can change in value, and the ability to exchange an asset depends on the available market. A displayed balance or estimated value is different from a completed sale or payment.

For a programme involving committed funds, read the conditions for holding, releasing, returning, or using them. Consider the amount committed, any costs, and the consequences of an interruption or an unfavourable outcome.

### Participation and result handling

Read how the experience checks activity, resolves outcomes, and handles incomplete or disputed records. Scores, points, credits, allocations, and payments each have their own meaning.

[Security & Fairness](/guides/security-and-fairness) explains the records and review questions behind those distinctions.

### Terms and access changes

Availability and participation conditions can change. Check which terms apply to an existing activity, how changes are communicated, and what happens to unfinished activity or funds if access ends.

Questions about legal requirements need the facts of the service and your circumstances. This guide does not establish a jurisdiction-specific policy.
