## Revenue Dashboard

The planned dashboard brings revenue, programme activity, and payment records into one view while keeping their sources and purposes visible.

A view would identify whose records it represents, the reporting period, the assets or units used, and when its information was last updated. A creator’s receipts, an audience allocation, and RNDRNTWRK’s platform revenue would remain separately labelled.

## Dashboard Overview

| Planned view | Useful information |
|---|---|
| **Revenue by source** | Revenue from the identified programme, service, settlement activity, or token-related source, with its period and status |
| **Balances and commitments** | Asset balances, amounts owed, funds committed to a purpose, and reserves |
| **Programme activity** | Streams, games, and other activities associated with the reporting period, including their recorded outcomes |
| **Community contributions** | Contributions and support associated with an activity, shown according to its measurement and visibility rules |

The underlying records would let a reader follow a summary back to its source, activity, recipient, and payment or programme reference.

## Keep Sources and Balances Distinct

Programme funding, service fees, settlement revenue, and token-related revenue need separate labels. Funds received to pay for work are tracked separately from revenue earned by delivering it.

A useful financial view distinguishes:

| Record | What it represents |
|---|---|
| **Gross source value** | The starting amount associated with the identified source |
| **Obligations and participant amounts** | Refunds, contractual payments, and amounts assigned or owed to creators, audiences, collaborators, or providers |
| **Recognised platform revenue** | The platform share of distributable source value after the source’s senior obligations |
| **Deployable platform surplus** | Recognised platform revenue after direct execution costs and realised loss provisions |
| **Balances, committed funds, and reserves** | Funds held or set aside for an identified purpose, rather than another revenue total |
| **Payment state** | What is pending, confirmed, returned, or still outstanding |

A balance can include funds owed to others. Funds committed to liquidity, settlement inventory, or a reserve also retain that purpose. These amounts need separate treatment from money spent on operating an activity.

Token-related fees follow their own source record and are recognised only when generated, claimed, and settled. Transaction volume and user principal are different from the fees earned by a service.

The [Economic Model](/economics) explains these distinctions. [Fee Distribution](/tokenomics/fee-distribution) sets out the planned source waterfalls and allocation bases.

## Updates and Alerts

The update design connects an event with its recorded status and reporting time. Examples include a tip, a subscription payment, an asset sale, or a programme payment where the relevant experience supports that activity.

Each update would identify the source and amount, the activity or recipient involved, and whether the record is newly reported, awaiting confirmation, completed, or corrected. The time of the activity, payment confirmation, and dashboard update may be different.

Useful alert topics include:

- A chosen revenue or activity milestone.
- An unusual change that needs review.
- An interrupted payment or service operation.
- A significant contribution under the programme’s reporting rules.
- A delayed update or unresolved difference between records.

An alert would point to the relevant record and its status so the reader can decide what to review next.

## Reports and Reconciliation

The reporting design includes daily summaries, weekly performance reviews, and longer-period comparisons. The reporting period is separate from a programme’s payout schedule.

A report would carry its sources, included activities, asset units, opening and closing balances, income and cost records, participant obligations, payments, reserves, and unresolved differences. If values are translated into another unit, it would identify the conversion source and time used.

Reconciliation connects those records: what was funded, earned, allocated, paid, returned, or left outstanding. Corrections need to remain associated with the records they change.

These summaries provide records for review. Formal financial statements and tax documentation need the appropriate accounting process and the underlying records.

## Programme Allocations and Claims

The planned programme view would distinguish an estimated amount, an allocation, a submitted claim, and a confirmed payout. Points and credits would retain the meaning given by the experience’s terms.

SW4P coordinates settlement: route selection, fees, execution, confirmation, recovery, and accounting for the result.

SW4P Earn extends SW4P’s economic model into source-aware allocation, claims, rewards, payout schedules, reserves, and reconciliation.

**Current status: SW4P Earn is not yet publicly available. No staking, liquidity, reward, or claim programme is currently open.**

Any future staking, reward, or claim programme would have its own eligible activity, funding, recipients, allocation rules, timing, and treatment of reserved or outstanding amounts. Fee-derived allocations and separately funded incentives would remain separately labelled in [SW4P Earn](/products/earn).
