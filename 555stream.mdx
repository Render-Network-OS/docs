555stream is a media environment for live and recorded programming, publishing, and interaction between people and agents.

A stream might follow a build, explain a product, present research, or bring a community into an ongoing programme. The work includes what happens on screen and what surrounds it: preparation, participation, recording, publication, and follow-up.

## Preparing a Stream

All you need to get started is an internet-enabled device. The [555stream site](https://stream.rndrntwrk.com) is the product destination.

The following preparation applies to the stream you want to run. Use the controls and connection options offered by your chosen setup.

### Destinations

Decide where people will watch. For an RTMP connection, the destination supplies a server address and stream key so your output reaches the correct channel. Keep stream keys private and check the destination's own access requirements.

Multi-destination output is part of the 555stream model. The destinations, output formats, and limits offered by a particular setup determine where that session can be sent.

### Scenes and Overlays

Prepare the layout, branding, captions, tickers, alerts, and sponsor material your programme needs. Decide how solo, multi-person, or picture-in-picture scenes should look and when to switch between them.

### Guests

Plan who will join and what they will contribute. Check their audio, video, and permissions before the session. Decide who handles invitations, scene changes, and individual audio controls.

### Check the Output

Review the picture, sound, overlays, and destination connection before beginning. During a session, use the available health information to watch for dropped frames, connection problems, or other interruptions.

### Between Streams

Plan any recorded programming, clips, games, or recurring activities that belong between live sessions. Assign responsibility for the schedule and check which scheduling or automation features your setup supports.

For desktop-encoder and hybrid workflows, see the [OBS Migration Guide](/guides/obs-migration). Use the source URL supplied by the relevant integration when adding a browser source.

## Sponsor Segments and L-Bar Layouts

An L-Bar places an advertising unit alongside the stream so both remain visible. Plan its dimensions, placement, duration, and behaviour on the devices your audience uses.

A sponsor segment needs agreed creative material, timing, and audience identification. The programme's terms establish what the sponsor receives and how the work is funded.

The media-routing design explores using programme context and audience activity to help choose placement timing. Creator permissions and the connected controls determine which changes an operator or agent may make.

Programme funding, service fees, settlement revenue, and token-related revenue have their own costs and allocation rules. See the [Economic Model](/economics) and [Fee Distribution](/tokenomics/fee-distribution) for how those sources are handled.

## Alice and Media Operations

RNDRNTWRK uses [Alice](/alice/persona) to develop and test its systems for research, planning, coding, and media operations.

A media assignment might involve preparing a production plan, organising assets, reviewing a recording, or coordinating a recurring programme. The integration design also includes session controls, scene changes, guests, schedules, and sponsor segments.

Each assignment needs an objective, permitted tools, operating limits, and a way to review the result. The [Intelligence Engine](/alice/intelligence) explains how context, tools, memory, and recovery support that work.

## Reliability and Recovery

A media session needs to account for its state before, during, and after an interruption.

The reliability design covers connection recovery, retained session state, output health, and handling media-processing failures. Region selection and fallback are infrastructure concerns; the available options and operating limits belong to the particular service configuration.

An operator needs to know which output stopped, what remains running, and whether the session can resume. Recording that sequence helps distinguish a recovered connection from a complete and usable recording.

## Architecture

The media architecture separates the following responsibilities:

| Component | Role in the design |
|---|---|
| **Browser client** | Capture, session interaction, and real-time communication. |
| **Media processing** | Encoding, storage, and queued production jobs. |
| **Control plane** | Scene and room state, source routing, configuration, and synchronisation. |
| **Multi-party communication** | Forward audio and video between participants; an SFU is a service for selectively forwarding those streams. |
| **Media engine** | Compose sources and transcode outputs. |
| **Recording and publishing** | Capture sessions, prepare clips and transcripts, publish outputs, and retain archives. |

These responsibilities connect production with distribution and audience interaction.

## Longer-Term Distribution

Decentralised streaming remains a long-term direction. It explores peer-assisted distribution, resilient ingest, and community-operated infrastructure.

Those ideas concern how media could be distributed and operated over time. They are separate from the connection options available in a particular stream setup.
