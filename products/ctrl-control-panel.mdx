The CTRL Panel design brings the controls for a creator experience into one place: scenes, overlays, participation, schedules, and sponsor material. It connects those controls to [555stream](/555stream) and other RNDRNTWRK products.

## Stream Production

A stream needs a way to organise its sources, choose what appears on screen, and follow the output.

The control-panel model covers session start and stop, scene changes, source routing, guest management, and output health. Decide who will operate these controls and how the session should be handled if a source or destination connection is interrupted.

Use the controls and connection options offered by your chosen stream setup.

## Presentation and Overlays

Prepare the visual elements around the stream:

- Themes, colours, fonts, and layouts.
- Backgrounds, lower-thirds, tickers, and captions.
- Static, animated, or video overlays.
- Entrance effects, time on screen, and exit transitions.
- Solo, multi-person, and picture-in-picture scenes.

Review how these elements fit together at the intended output size. The [Animated Assets](/products/animated-assets) page explains the planned library and how to prepare motion assets.

## Games and Participation

The participation model connects a stream with games, challenges, quests, and other interactive activities.

Choose activities that fit the audience and the programme. Explain how people join, what they do, and how results are followed. Plan when an activity starts and ends, where it appears in the stream, and which controls its operator needs.

[555 Arcade](/arcade/overview) describes the interactive environments; each experience determines the activities it offers.

## Sponsor Material and Campaign Controls

Sponsor material can take the form of a badge, lower-third, ticker, sidebar, L-Bar layout, full-screen placement, or a segment within the programme.

For a proposed placement, define:

- The creative assets, dimensions, and accepted content.
- Its entrance, time on screen, exit, and any scene change.
- How often it appears and which times or programme segments it belongs to.
- How different creative versions rotate and how their results will be compared.
- Whether a countdown, audio-level change, or handover is needed around a commercial break.
- The fallback material and who may pause or change the placement.

The campaign-control design also covers budgets, spending schedules, content suitability, reporting, and payment records. The [Monetization Module](/products/monetization-module) explains those commercial arrangements.

## Collaborators and Collectives

A programme may involve several creators or a collective. Plan shared placements, schedules, responsibilities, and payment terms together.

A media kit can explain the formats offered, the audience or community involved, and the basis for any reported results. Use the same brief and placement specifications when coordinating work across contributors.
